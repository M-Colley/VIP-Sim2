Accessibility simulation

Experience your design through impaired vision.

VIP-Sim overlays a live simulation of vision impairments on any application already running on your desktop. You keep using that application normally — clicking, scrolling, typing — while the simulation runs on top of it.

Built to be used on real software

Most simulators show you a photograph. VIP-Sim shows you your own work, live, while you use it.

Click-through overlay

The simulation sits on top of the desktop without intercepting your input. The application underneath behaves exactly as it always does.

Follows your gaze

Effects that depend on where you are looking track the mouse by default, or your actual gaze via the webcam after a short calibration.

One symptom at a time

Eighteen effects, each approximating a single symptom, combinable and adjustable — so you can tell which one your interface fails.

Explains itself

Every symptom is described in plain language beside its clinical term, in the app. Press F1.

Multi-monitor

Move the overlay to whichever display the work is on. It remembers.

Private by construction

Screen and camera images are processed on your machine and never leave it. No accounts, no analytics.

On Linux, capture goes through your desktop's screen-sharing dialogue; Sway and other wlroots compositors can only share a whole screen, so give VIP-Sim a different monitor from the one it is overlaying. Mouse-following cannot work there — Wayland does not let an application read the pointer position — so gaze uses the webcam. macOS asks for Screen Recording permission on first launch, and Camera only if you enable eye tracking. Builds are not yet code-signed, so the first launch needs an extra confirmation — the steps are in the README that ships with the download.

From research, and honest about its limits

VIP-Sim was built and evaluated with and for people with visual impairments, and published at UIST '25.

What VIP-Sim is not

It is a design and awareness tool, not a medical device. It does not diagnose, screen for or monitor any condition. Severity values are uncalibrated starting points rather than clinically validated measurements, so VIP-Sim should never be presented as showing “what condition X looks like”, and it is not evidence of accessibility compliance.

Take part in the research

A study at University College London into what changes when designers use a vision-impairment simulation: what they do differently, and what they come to believe. Run by UCL, separately from the product.

The awkward question we are asking

There is real evidence that disability simulations can backfire — several studies find that afterwards people rate disabled people as less capable and feel less comfortable around them. VIP-Sim should be measured against that rather than assumed to be exempt. This study is designed to find out whether it helps, does nothing, or does harm, and we will publish the answer either way.

Who we are looking for

People who design or build software: designers, developers, researchers, students. No accessibility experience needed — we are just as interested in people who have never thought about it.

What taking part involves

A short questionnaire, a design task using VIP-Sim, and a follow-up. Remote and in your own time. We tell you the exact time commitment before you decide.

What we measure

Which accessibility problems you find and fix, and your attitudes towards disability before and after, using established scales.

Your data

Handled under the study's ethics approval, kept separately from the mailing list, and reported only in aggregate. You can withdraw at any point without giving a reason.

Interested? Email m.colley@ucl.ac.uk and we will send you the participant information sheet. That is not consent to take part: you read it, then you decide, and you are free to say no.

Deliberately by email rather than through the signup form above, so that interest in a study never touches a product mailing list. Support

Report a problem

Open an issue at github.com/M-Colley/VIP-Sim2/issues. There is a button for it in the app, under F1.

Attach diagnostics

Press F1 and choose Copy diagnostics path. Attach Player.log and vipsim-errors.log from that folder.

Emergency exit

Ctrl+Alt+Q always quits, even if the overlay has covered everything.
